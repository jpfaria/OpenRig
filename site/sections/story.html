How it started

This started because three
multi-effects units cost a fortune.

My son started guitar lessons. His teacher had one sound, we had another one at home, and what he learned in the lesson didn't happen again afterwards. Fixing that by buying everyone the same unit cost more than the instrument.

I've been a developer for 26 years. At the time I was investigating, with method, how far you can go programming with AI — and a study like that is only worth anything if the problem is hard. Live audio is hard: the code runs a few hundred times a second and, when it gets it wrong, the sound fails right there, in front of everyone. There's no hiding it.

It worked out better than I expected. OpenRig today is a real-time audio platform written in Rust: every input is an isolated runtime, so two guitars on the same interface really are two independent rigs. Amp, cab and pedal mixing native DSP, neural captures and IRs in the same chain. Presets in plain text you can open and edit.

And the original idea grew with it: instead of one tone for me and my son, one computer on stage processing the whole band, each musician working on their own sound. Only one of them needs the gear.
